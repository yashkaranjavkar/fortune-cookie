# Fortunery — Fortune Cookie Inspector

**Genre:** Cozy inspection / sorting puzzle · narrative workplace sim · "Papers, Please" meets a bakery
**Platforms:** PC (Windows/macOS via desktop app) and web browser
**Session length:** 20–30 minutes per full run, built for short replayable levels
**Price idea:** $4.99 (launch discount $3.99) · free web demo covering Level 1

---

## The pitch

Welcome to the fortune cookie factory. You've just been hired as the newest **Fortune Cookie Inspector**, and every cookie that rolls off the line has a fortune inside, and some of those fortunes are lying to you.

Crack open cookies, read each fortune, and decide in seconds whether it belongs in the **Approved** tray or the **Faulty** tray. Then walk to the inspection room, turn on your torch, and highlight exactly *what* made each fortune faulty. Your paycheck depends on it.

It looks like a warm, hand-drawn bakery game. Underneath, it quietly teaches you to spot the scam links you see every day.

## Core mechanic

1. **Crack and sort.** Cookies arrive under glass domes. Hover to lift the dome, crack the cookie, drag the paper slip into Approved or Faulty. A timer dial counts down.
2. **Read the URL.** Every fortune contains a web link. Is it really `youtube.com`, or is it `youtube.corn`, `yourtube.com` or `youttube.com`? One wrong letter means the fortune is faulty.
3. **Mark and inspect.** In the inspection room, use a highlighter to mark the exact part of the fortune that gives it away, and slide a torch across the slips to check your work.
4. **Get paid (or docked).** Every correct sort adds to your incentive, and every miss deducts from it. Earn up to three stars per level.

### Progression
- **Level 1 (4 cookies):** Is the URL real or fake? Learn the basics of typosquatting.
- **Level 2 (5 cookies):** Context matters. A real streaming link inside a fortune about banking is suspicious. A *fake* link is fine if the fortune is **warning** you away from it. Inviting you to click it makes it a phishing attempt.
- **Level 3 (6 cookies):** Tighter timers and trickier look-alike domains, built around a bulk order for a farewell party.
- **Supervisor round:** You're promoted. Review your interns' sorting decisions, read their reasoning, and confirm or overturn each call. Your verdict sets the factory's revenue.
- **Final trays:** A last speed-sorting rush to close the shift.

## Art style and feel
- Hand-drawn, warm illustration on paper textures: cookies, trays, hands and a factory floor plan you walk across
- A friendly mascot who pops up with hints and wisecracks and never covers the buttons you need
- Custom cursors: a gloved hand to grab slips, a fist to drag, a highlighter and a pen for marking
- Cinematic room transitions: the camera follows you across the factory map and zooms into the next room
- A soft, bakery-like soundscape of cookie cracks, footsteps, highlighter squeaks and coin clinks. Wrong answers get a gentle thud, never an alarm.
- A reduced-motion mode for accessibility

## Optional personalisation
An optional companion browser extension can, with your explicit opt-in consent, learn which sites you actually use (host names only, stored locally, deletable at any time) so that fortunes feature brands you recognise. The game is fully playable without it.

## Who it's for
- Fans of cozy inspection and sorting games (*Papers, Please*, *Unpacking*, *Strange Horticulture*, *Potion Craft*)
- Puzzle players who like "spot the difference" and attention-to-detail challenges
- People who want to get better at spotting scams and phishing links without sitting through a lecture
- Parents, teachers and teams looking for a short, playful security-awareness experience (it was originally built for corporate security training at an IT services company)

## Key features
- 3 levels plus a supervisor review and a final speed round, each with distinct rules
- Real-world phishing patterns: typosquatting, swapped letters, wrong top-level domains and misleading context
- Highlight-the-flaw inspection, so you show *why* a fortune is wrong, not just that it is
- An incentive and star system that rewards accuracy under time pressure
- Short sessions, with replay balloons to retry a level

## Honest notes
- Content is focused on URL and link safety. This is a short, polished experience, not a 20-hour game.
- English only at launch.
